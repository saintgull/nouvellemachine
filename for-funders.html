Nouvelle Machine

Paris's front door into AI safety — a brief for funders

Paris has world-class AI safety institutions and is still lacking a front door, which I am building through Nouvelle Machine: a weekly bilingual dinner, and a Saturday build club (called Banc d'Essai) that intends to turn curious newcomers into people doing real AI-safety work, then route them to organizations that fit their interest and highest possible impact.

Goals

The goal is to convert latent French talent into people who have touched AI-safety work and been adeptly routed onward to the institutions that can use them. Concretely, by the end of six months I am aiming for:

25+
regulars
15+
first projects shipped
8+
confirmed onward routings

Onward routings go into organizations like CeSIA, BlueDot, ML4Good or a governance track. I have been tracking these numbers from day one, and they will be reported publicly at 3 and 6 months.

How it works

Nouvelle Machine has two linked formats, both live since the September rentrée.

Dîner Machine is a weekly bilingual (FR/EN) tech dinner. It's the top of the funnel: it gathers open, capable people (engineers, students, career-changers) who don't yet identify with AI safety. Each dinner has a theme or rotating edition: a papers night, a game-theory night, a predictions night, and so on. I believe bilingual-by-default can make a real difference, in a scene that otherwise splits into francophone-institutional and anglophone-expat with almost no bridge.

Banc d'Essai is the Saturday build club, with the first session planned for October 17th. The format is a short accessible demo to open (run a small eval, read a transcript of a model scheming), then cowork blocks where each person starts a first small project: an eval, a French translation of a key resource (I will especially focus on those at the start, for further outreach), a short governance brief, a rebuttal post. I keep a project menu to prevent the failure mode of people not knowing which project to start and leaving without first-hand experience.

My thinking on routing has moved since the spring. I used to pitch one of the outcomes as producing future junior technical researchers, and I don't believe that framing anymore, as I now believe governance is much higher-impact than technical safety. Doing a small project, as I see it, is mostly the fastest way to actually understand the problem and to feel it's yours to work on. I think AI safety needs to become a broad cause the way climate is one: it needs technical people, but also governance, policy, communication and an informed public around all of them. So governance and comms are explicit exit paths in how I route people, and I will likely prioritize those at the start, depending on the profiles.

I focus on entry-level routing, which makes me a feeder, not a rival, of the existing institutions, many of which I am on good terms with (CeSIA, ML4Good, and the upcoming impact hub in Paris). I intend to use my network to identify competent people and put them in contact with the institutions that best fit their areas of interest and their highest leverage for impact.

If it works it becomes a replicable kit, which I am interested in moving toward eventually in Europe, notably because of how neglected the middle powers have been in the AI safety conversation so far.

How the funding is used

I'm asking for €17,400 to €23,100 (roughly $20,000 to $27,000) for six months, depending on how much of the operations budget other funders cover. About 75% is my salary (6 × €2,900/month, self-employed, includes ~22% French cotisations), which is around 70% of my Paris counterfactual salary. The rest is operations: the build club (food, room, compute for demos and attendee projects), a Demo Day where people present what they built, and an ~8% buffer. Attendees pay for their own dinner at Dîner Machine, so there's no food line for the weekly dinners. The minimal version is salary-only, if the operations lines are covered by Essaim or a small BlueDot grant.

What I'd do without this funding

Without funding I take a day job. My live applications are for community and events roles in Paris, I've applied to ML4Good in communications, and I'm exploring event organizing for EA France, all of which are the same goal by a different route. If those fail I would most likely return to language education, which is time-intensive and, sadly, low-impact for the causes I care about. The dinner would probably survive at hobby scale, but there would be no Banc d'Essai conversion work, no project menu, no metrics, and no kit.

What I'd do with more

More funding would go into deepening the francophone outreach and starting to make this outlast Paris, not into a bigger team. Concretely: a French-translation program paying people to translate key AI-safety resources, scaling the newsletter and short-form video, and seeding a replication in a second European city such as Brussels or Lausanne. More of the outreach and the replicable kit, rather than more headcount.

Who is on the team, and track record

It's just me, and I have built this kind of community from zero before. In Toronto I ran a weekly ritual that grew into a community of around 100 people, plus an events-aggregation platform and one-off formats (demo nights, a cohesion summit, teaching at Weave). I launched AI-safety meetups from zero in under two weeks each in Kraków and Warsaw. I attended 110+ events in a year with systematic notes, so I know empirically what makes a room work. I'm a native FR/EN bilingual, resident in Paris, in contact with CeSIA and around the Pause IA France, EA France, and ACX/LW crowds. I build my own tooling, including a Paris event scraper and classifier, plus my own CRMs and outreach automation. On the same thesis I am launching a French-language AI Safety Digest newsletter, have started short-form French AI-safety video, and wrote a manifesto article on making AI safety a mainstream cause through education.

Risks and failure modes

The most likely failure, and the core risk given the thesis, is that people come but don't convert: the dinner gathers a nice crowd but few of them ship a first project. I'm addressing it with a small-granularity project menu (2 to 3 hours) and hands-on help in the room, but it's the hypothesis the first three months will actually test, and I'll report the real numbers either way. The second risk is Paris retention, since the city is saturated with events and the crowd is volatile; I recruit in person at existing events and launched at the rentrée, the best window of the year. The third is the single point of failure: the whole project so far depends on one person, me. If it fails, the outcome is mild and cheap: the dinner shrinks to hobby scale or merges into an existing event, fewer francophones get routed in, and I publish an honest write-up of what didn't work so the next person doesn't repeat it.

Funding raised so far

None so far. Essaim, the francophone EA funding circle, has decided to fund this project; the exact amount is being finalized and expected within days. Nothing else has been raised in the last 12 months. I'm also applying to the LTFF and the Astralis/Macroscopic Middle Powers RFP, and I'll disclose the Essaim figure and reduce overlapping asks as soon as it's confirmed.
